Readings

This article made it so clear to me that the idea of nuclear war has seemed like such a distant threat that has such low possibilities of happening could actually happen in my lifetime. Nuclear weapons were usually talked about in my history classes so I never really considered the possibility of actually experiencing something like that. What scared me the most was learning about how seriously the U.S. and other governments are preparing for the possibility of nuclear war - it's something that's actually being prepared for and not something that can just be brushed off as a thing that will never happen (at least as long as we're alive). The article explains that Ukraine has identified nearly 200 hospitals to treat people in the event of a nuclear attack - seeing that kind of preparation taking place makes the threat feel so much more real. I found the article's interactive visuals super effective in getting across the potential consequences of nuclear war. Rather than relying entirely on statistics and political videos, the interactive visuals help readers imagine the severity of the destruction that could happen. There is something so unsettling about being able to actually see the information presented instead of just reading about what a nuclear weapon could do. The graphics really encourage the reader to stop and think about if this actually became a reality. I do think the interactive visuals are one of the article's strongest qualities because it makes such a distant threat feel so real. Another detail that stood out to me was the article's explanation of nuclear deterrence. The idea, for decades, has been that countries avoid using nuclear weapons because they understand that using them could result in an equally tragic response. The article says that in 2022, American intelligence estimated there was a 50/50 chance that Russia would use a tactical nuclear weapon if Ukrainian forces managed to advance toward Crimea. That stat was so alarming to me because it shows that nuclear escalation was not a concern 'in theory', but government officials were actually preparing for that possibility. This article left me feeling so uneasy, but also way more aware of how important international cooperation is. I think it is very easy to become desensitized to international conflicts when they are constantly shown to us as headlines. This article reminded me that behind those headlines are consequences that could affect the entire world in the worst ways. Nuclear war may seem distant, but this article definitely taught me that the possibility is not something we should become comfortable ignoring.
